# Plan forward — 2026-08-30

Written because the evening turned into a loop of theories. Everything below is
ordered so that each step **removes a variable**, and the two steps that need
parts are separated from the ones that do not.

## What is actually established

Not opinion — each of these was measured today.

| Fact | Evidence |
|---|---|
| The Pico is **not fried** | It enters BOOTSEL, accepts an 843 KB UF2, enumerates USB and prints |
| `clk0` above ~1 V ⇒ the board draws 2+ A | **0.77 V → 0.24 A; 1.76 V → 2.5 A**, straddling the 0.8–1.5 V Vgs(th) band |
| That is the `cclk` precharge chain | `clk0` → Q2229/Q2420 → n519/n358 → n1129/n1467 → Q432/Q3504 → `cclk` → **32 FETs**, two inversions, non-inverting. `docs/clk0-pulldown.md` |
| 16 of those 32 have no series resistor | `idb0-7`, `sb0-7`; **14.7–16.0% duty** under real code (`tools/contention_duty.py --halves 600`) |
| Nothing is shorted | 0.24 A rests below the 0.548 A passive ceiling; unpowered meter check matches the 2026-08-12 baseline |
| Duty cycle alone does not save it | The 9% asymmetric build still died — brownout is **peak**-limited |
| **A serial port exists only while the board draws 2.5 A** | User observation, this evening — the Pico runs *and* the board draws 2.5 A together, and both stop together |

That last line is the one that reframes everything: it is a **boot loop**, not a
dead board. The Pico boots, something makes the board draw 2.5 A, the rail
folds, the module resets, the port vanishes, the board falls back to 0.24 A —
and round again. The ~2 Hz S1 blink seen on 2026-08-29 is this loop made visible.

## Step 1 — remove the bench supply from the picture (no parts, 5 minutes)

**Board supply OFF. Power the whole thing from USB alone**, through the Pico's
VSYS (pin 39 is soldered, so USB already feeds board VCC — this is not a
modification, just unplugging one thing).

A quiet board is 0.24 A and a Mac port will carry that. So:

- **It comes up, holds a link, serves the setup AP** ⇒ the bench supply is the
  fault, not the board. Everything since 12:00 is a power-delivery problem.
- **It still boot-loops** ⇒ the board really does demand 2.5 A the moment the
  Pico runs, and Step 2 is compulsory before anything else.

This is worth doing first because it is free and it splits the remaining
possibilities in half.

## Step 2 — a firmware that provably never clocks (no parts)

```
python3 tools/pico_flash.py usbonly     # no BOOTSEL button needed
```

`selftest` built with `-DUSB_ONLY=ON` calls `stdio_init_all()` and nothing else —
no `bus_init`, no GPIO driven, no radio. It printed **29 clean lines over 14.5 s**
on 2026-08-29 when every other build died in milliseconds.

- **Link holds** ⇒ Pico, cable, port and rail are all fine when the board is not
  being driven, and the fault is entirely in what the firmware does to the board.
- **Link dies** ⇒ the rail cannot support even an idle Pico, which points at
  supply wiring rather than at the 6502.

## Step 3 — the two hardware fixes, in this order

**3a. Replace the 31 kΩ clk0 pull-down with 4.7 kΩ.** Measured today: something
sources 25–57 µA into that node, and 31 k turns that into 1.76 V — above
threshold, which is the 2.5 A state. 4.7 k gives 0.12–0.27 V parked and still
2.72 V when driven, against a 1.5 V worst-case threshold.
**The 47 kΩ recommended in `docs/clk0-pulldown.md` was wrong** and is corrected
there; it assumed the node's only load was two FET gates drawing nothing.

**3b. The 16 remaining precharge sites** — `idb0-7` and `sb0-7`, 10 kΩ in series
with pin 3, the same operation done twice already. Map with positions and
neighbours: `docs/rework-precharge-marked.jpg`
(`tools/mark_rework_precharge.py`). Worth **~0.65 A** off the executing current
at the measured 15% duty. Start with `idb7` (Q1830) — the one the thermal camera
found.

**3c. Bulk capacitance, ~500 µF low-ESR across VCC/VSS (TP36/TP35).** The
asymmetric clock cuts average current but not the peak, and today proved the
peak is what folds the rail. This is the part that makes short high phases
survivable.

## Step 4 — only then, a verdict

```
python3 tools/pico_flash.py selftest    # 23 subtests, prints its own verdict
```

Expect `SELFTEST pass 1: $0480 *** ALL 23 SUBTESTS PASSED ***`. Then the wifi
build for the panel, then Klaus Dormann's suite as the acceptance gate.

## What NOT to do again

- **Do not attach a terminal to the tester firmware while hoping to measure.**
  It stops clocking on attach and a parked clock was the high-current state
  before the pull-down existed. That is what the selftest firmware is for.
- **Do not chase this with more firmware variants.** Three builds have now died
  at three different points and each time the cause was the rail.
- **Do not poll a long run over wifi** — lwIP wedges; read the verdict on USB.
- **Watch the epoxy on the reworked sites.** It is insulating, but a lifted
  pin 3 pressed back onto its pad shorts out the series resistor and silently
  reverts that site. The 16 already done should be **cold** on the FLIR; if any
  is hot, that is what happened.
