  • Surface finish: ENIG — the croc-clip bond pads see repeated contact and HASL wears; the quoted €131 PCB line is with ENIG, so the saving from HASL is small here
  • Impedance control: No · default 6-layer stackup is fine
  • Order number: pay ~$1.5 to remove, or JLC prints theirs on the die face
  • Min via hole size: choose 0.2mm/(0.3/0.35mm) (~+€25). Every via here is a 0.2 mm hole in a 0.45 mm pad; the default 0.3 mm class would enlarge them and cut the annular ring from 0.125 to 0.075 mm. Verified safe either way — closest hole pair on the board is 0.520 mm centre-to-centre, so hole-to-hole stays ≥0.22 mm even at 0.3 mm — but with 14,454 vias on a large 6-layer board, and 3,817 of them inside SMD pads (epoxy filled & capped), the thicker ring is cheap insurance against a single hard-to-find open.
  • Via Covering: keep Epoxy Filled & Capped — not an upsell here: 3,817 vias sit on SMD pad copper (the router terminates stubs inside their own pad), and unfilled via-in-pad wicks solder away from the joint during reflow.
  • JLCPCB adds two 5 mm edge rails for assembly, so the quote reads 300.7 × 322 mm — expected, not an error (290.7 + 2×5). Plane copper stops 0.50 mm from the outline, fine for scoring or routing. Decide whether to pay for depaneling rather than snapping a 290 mm 6-layer board by hand.

Our data, for comparison against their preview:
  • Q1–Q4051 SOT-323 @ 0°, top: pad 1 Gate at (−0.887, −0.650) = upper-left, pad 2 Source lower-left, pad 3 Drain right-middle
  • D1–D55 LED 0603 @ 0°, top: pad 1 = cathode
  • D56–D67 1N4148WS SOD-323 @ 180°, bottom: pad 1 = cathode (clamp-up cathode→VCC, clamp-down cathode→signal)
  • Pico area must show only 26 resistors — no module
The 3D preview is not evidence. JLCPCB state the online previewer is reference-only and their engineer verifies position, rotation and polarity — their viewer places its own library model at its own orientation, so a body drawn 180° out is normal there. Judge from the parts-placement preview, not the 3D view.
Where the real risk lies: a 180°-rotated SOT-323 physically cannot sit on our pads (two leads over the single right pad), so a systematic FET flip is glaring. The dangerous parts are the 2-pin polarised ones — the 55 LEDs and 12 clamp diodes — where 180° looks perfectly fine and is electrically fatal. Check one FET gate, one LED cathode, one clamp diode; if those three are right, all 5,328 are.
